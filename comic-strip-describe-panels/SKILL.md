# Comic Strip Panel Descriptions

Write technical, actionable panel descriptions that give artists a clear blueprint without micromanaging.

## Core Principle

Panel descriptions are **technical instructions, not prose**. You're giving an artist a blueprint, not writing a novel.

## Essential Constraints

| Constraint | Guideline | Why It Matters |
|------------|-----------|----------------|
| Length | 1–2 sentences per panel | Artists need clarity, not paragraphs |
| Frozen moment | One instant in time | Comics capture single frames, not continuous action |
| Specificity | Story-critical details only | Trust the artist for the rest |

## The Description Formula

```
PANEL [#]: [SHOT TYPE]. [LOCATION/TIME if new]. [Main action, described left-to-right].
    [CHARACTER NAME]: "[Dialogue]"
    [SFX]: [Sound effect if needed]
```

### Example

```
PANEL 3: CLOSE-UP. Rain-streaked window in background.
    Dog's face fills the frame, ears drooped, eyes wide.
    DOG (thought balloon): "Betrayal."
```

## Shot Terminology

Agree on these terms with your artist before starting:

| Shot Type | What It Shows | When to Use |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **ESTABLISHING SHOT** / **WIDE SHOT** | Full environment, characters small | Panel 1 context, location changes, scale |
| **MEDIUM SHOT** | Characters from waist up | Conversations, group interactions |
| **CLOSE-UP** | Face or single object fills frame | Emotional beats, focal points |
| **EXTREME CLOSE-UP** | Detail (eyes, hands, specific object) | Emphasis, tension, punchline details |
| **OVER-THE-SHOULDER** | From behind one character toward another | Conversations, POV moments |
| **BIRD'S EYE VIEW** | Looking straight down | Disorientation, overview, god's-eye perspective |
| **WORM'S EYE VIEW** | Looking straight up | Intimidation, scale, dramatic angle |

**Camera angle affects emotion:**
- Eye level = neutral
- Low angle (looking up) = powerful, intimidating
- High angle (looking down) = vulnerable, small

## Description Principles

### 1. One Frozen Moment Per Panel

Comics capture instants, not sequences.

**Don't write:**
> "She opens the door and walks inside."

**Do write:**
> "She stands in the doorway, hand on the frame, peering into darkness."

**Specify the exact point in an action:**

| Vague | Specific Frozen Moment |
|-------|------------------------|
| "He jumps" | "He's at the apex of the jump, arms reaching for the ledge" |
| "They fight" | "Her fist connects with his jaw, his head snapping sideways" |
| "She falls" | "She's midair, arms windmilling, eyes wide" |
| "He drinks coffee" | "Cup at lips, eyes closed, steam rising" |

### 2. Describe Foreground to Background, Left to Right

This mirrors how artists draw and readers scan.

**Example:**
> "PANEL 2: MEDIUM SHOT. Coffee shop interior.
> Left foreground: Emma leans across the table, gesturing emphatically.
> Right: Marcus slouches back in his chair, arms crossed.
> Background: Other patrons blur out of focus."

### 3. Main Focal Point First

What should the reader's eye go to immediately? Describe that element first, then supporting details.

**Example:**
> "PANEL 4: CLOSE-UP. The letter 'REJECTED' in bold red ink dominates the frame. Character's trembling hand holds the bottom edge."

### 4. Only Describe What Matters

If an element doesn't affect plot, mood, or joke, delete it.

**Overwritten:**
> "A brown tabby cat with white paws sits on a blue cushion near a window with white curtains. The cat is meowing. There's a potted plant on the windowsill."

**Right:**
> "Cat meowing at window."

The artist will make it look good. You don't need to specify every detail.

### 5. Position Characters in Speaking Order

The first speaker should be on the left so word balloon tails don't cross awkwardly.

**Example:**
> "PANEL 2: MEDIUM SHOT. Left: Jane faces right. Right: Tom faces left.
> JANE: 'Where were you?'
> TOM: 'Traffic.'"

Readers scan left-to-right, so balloons naturally flow.

### 6. Trust the Artist

Don't specify every detail of composition, expression, or styling unless it's story-critical.

**Micromanaging:**
> "She smiles, showing teeth, with a slight dimple on her left cheek, eyes crinkled at the corners, head tilted 15 degrees to the right."

**Trusting:**
> "She smiles, relieved."

Artists interpret emotion. You're the writer, not the art director.

## Describing Emotion and Expression

Use clear, specific emotional states rather than vague descriptions:

| Vague | Specific |
|-------|----------|
| "He looks upset" | "He scowls, jaw clenched" or "He looks away, blinking rapidly" |
| "She's happy" | "She grins, eyes bright" or "She laughs, head thrown back" |
| "They're worried" | "They exchange nervous glances" or "Sweat beads on his forehead" |

**Physical manifestations > emotional labels**

## Sound Effects and Typography

### When to Include SFX

- **Action clarity:** CRASH, BANG, SLAM for impact
- **Environmental:** TICK TOCK, DRIP, BUZZ for ambiance
- **Comedic timing:** BOING, SPLAT, WHOOSH for cartoon physics

### SFX Format

```
SFX: CRUNCH
```

Or integrated into description:
> "PANEL 3: The vase shatters mid-air (SFX: CRASH), shards frozen in explosion pattern."

### Typography Cues

Only specify if story-critical:
- Emphasis: "I SAID no"
- Whispers: Smaller text, lighter balloon
- Thoughts: Thought balloon vs. speech balloon
- Off-panel: Tailless balloon pointing from edge

## Common Description Mistakes

| Mistake | What Goes Wrong | The Fix |
|---------|-----------------|---------|
| **Continuous action** | "He walks across the room and sits down" | Pick one frozen instant: "He's mid-stride" OR "He sinks into chair" |
| **Micromanaging** | Specifying every pose detail, expression nuance | Describe only story-critical elements; trust the artist |
| **Talking heads** | Every panel is characters standing still, talking | Add action, movement, varied shots, silent panels |
| **Unclear focal point** | Everything described equally | Lead with what matters most; reader's eye follows your description order |
| **Camera changes mid-panel** | "Close-up of his face, then we see her across the room" | One shot per panel; change shots between panels |
| **Describing dialogue content** | "He tells her about the plan" + separate dialogue | Just write the dialogue; don't describe it too |

## Special Cases

### Silent Panels

```
PANEL 2: CLOSE-UP. Her hand trembles, hovering over the phone.
    [No dialogue]
```

Silence is powerful. Sometimes the best panel has no words.

### Establishing Shots

```
PANEL 1: ESTABLISHING SHOT. Urban skyline at dusk, lights beginning to glow.
```

Sets location and time without characters. Usually panel 1 or after location change.

### Montage/Passage of Time

```
PANEL 2: SERIES OF SMALL IMAGES: Clock showing different times, empty coffee cups piling up, character's beard growing longer.
```

For time passage in limited space.

### Off-Panel Action

```
PANEL 4: Character reacts with shock, looking off-panel right.
    [OFF-PANEL]: CRASH!
    CHARACTER: "Not again..."
```

## Panel Description Checklist

Before finalizing descriptions:

- [ ] Each description is 1–2 sentences?
- [ ] Specified one frozen moment per panel (not continuous action)?
- [ ] Described foreground to background, left to right?
- [ ] Main focal point described first?
- [ ] Characters positioned in speaking order (left speaks first)?
- [ ] Removed details that don't affect story/mood/joke?
- [ ] Trusted the artist with non-critical details?
- [ ] Shot type specified for each panel?
- [ ] Location noted when it changes?

## Output Format

Standard panel script format:

```
STRIP TITLE: [Title]

PANEL 1: [SHOT TYPE]. [Location/time if needed].
    [Description: foreground to background, left to right, one frozen moment].
    CHARACTER NAME: "[Dialogue]"
    [SFX]: [Sound] (if needed)

PANEL 2: [SHOT TYPE]. [New location/time if changed].
    [Description].
    CHARACTER: "[Dialogue]"

[Continue for all panels...]
```

```
PANEL #: [SHOT]. [Location if new]. [Action: frozen moment, L-R].

```
