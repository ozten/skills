# Phase 2: Brand Positioning

**Goal:** Derive the brand's emotional territory and positioning from GTM inputs. This is the bridge between strategy and creative — it determines *what emotional space* the brand occupies before we explore *how* it shows up.

**Reference:** `references/tone-archetypes.md` — for archetype options and ICP-fit guidance.

## Process

### Step 1: Identify the Emotional Core

Every product solves a functional problem. But people buy based on how the *solution makes them feel*. Derive the emotional territory from the ICP's pain:

**Framework: Pain → Fear → Relief → Identity**

| Layer | Question | Example (repair tracker) |
|-------|----------|--------------------------|
| Pain | What's broken? | "I can't find my repair receipts at tax time" |
| Fear | What's the worst case? | "I'm leaving money on the table / IRS trouble" |
| Relief | What does solved feel like? | "Everything's in one place, I'm not scrambling" |
| Identity | Who do they become? | "The landlord who has their shit together" |

The **Identity** layer is where brand positioning lives. You're not selling repair tracking — you're selling the feeling of being organized, in control, competent.

Present this as:

```
## Emotional Territory

**Pain:** [functional pain]
**Fear underneath:** [what they're really afraid of]
**Relief looks like:** [the emotional state after solving]
**Identity shift:** [who they become with your product]

→ The brand should make the user feel: **[1-2 word emotional territory]**
```

### Step 2: Derive Brand Tension

Great brands live in a productive tension — they balance two qualities that seem opposed. This tension creates energy and memorability.

Pick the tension that maps to the ICP's world:

| ICP Psychology | Brand Tension | Example Products |
|----------------|---------------|------------------|
| Overwhelmed, non-technical | **Simple ↔ Powerful** | Basecamp, Linear |
| Cost-conscious, DIY | **Scrappy ↔ Reliable** | Mailchimp (early), Carrd |
| Professional but small | **Serious ↔ Approachable** | Stripe, Mercury |
| Skeptical of software | **Trustworthy ↔ Modern** | Vanguard, SimplePractice |
| Time-poor, action-oriented | **Fast ↔ Thorough** | Superhuman, Raycast |

Present:

```
## Brand Tension

**[Quality A] ↔ [Quality B]**

Your ICP is [description]. They need to feel [Quality A] to trust you
and [Quality B] to choose you over [current alternative].
```

### Step 3: Map Anti-Positioning to Creative Boundaries

The anti-positioning from the GTM decomposition creates hard boundaries for the brand:

```
## Creative Boundaries (from anti-positioning)

**We are NOT:**
- [anti-position 1] → So the brand should NEVER feel like [implication]
- [anti-position 2] → So the brand should NEVER feel like [implication]
- [anti-position 3] → So the brand should NEVER feel like [implication]
```

Example:
- "Not a PM suite" → Brand should NEVER feel enterprise, complex, or feature-heavy
- "Not accounting software" → Brand should NEVER feel clinical, numbers-obsessed, or financial
- "Not for 50+ units" → Brand should NEVER feel scaled, corporate, or impersonal

### Step 4: Identify the Archetype Range

Using `references/tone-archetypes.md`, select 3-5 archetypes that *could* work for this brand positioning. Not all will — that's what treatments will explore.

Present:

```
## Viable Archetypes

These archetypes fit the emotional territory and ICP:

1. **[Archetype Name]** — [1 sentence on why it fits]
2. **[Archetype Name]** — [1 sentence on why it fits]
3. **[Archetype Name]** — [1 sentence on why it fits]

Excluded: [Archetype] (too [reason]), [Archetype] (wrong for [ICP trait])
```

### Step 5: Present and Confirm

Show the complete brand positioning and ask for a quick gut check:

> This is the strategic foundation for all creative treatments. Does the emotional territory feel right? Anything feel off about the tension or boundaries?

At most 1 question. Don't block — if the user says "looks good," move on.

### Step 6: Gate Check

**Proceed to Phase 3 when:**
- Emotional territory is identified
- Brand tension is named
- At least 3 viable archetypes are listed

Transition:

> Brand position locked. Now let's explore **how this brand could show up** — 3-5 distinct creative treatments, each a different world.

Then read `workflows/03-treatments.md`.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Don't skip the emotional core.** Jumping straight to "let's pick colors" produces shallow, disconnectable creative.
- **Don't pick one archetype yet.** Phase 2 identifies the *range*. Phase 3 explores specific archetypes as treatments.
- **Don't make it academic.** "Emotional territory" sounds fancy — keep it grounded in the ICP's actual language and feelings.
- **Don't present 10 archetypes.** 3-5 viable ones is enough. Too many options paralyze.
- **Don't ignore anti-positioning.** The boundaries are as important as the direction — they prevent the brand from becoming generic.
