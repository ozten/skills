# Phase 4: Selection & Refinement

**Goal:** User selects a treatment (or hybrid), and we refine it into a confirmed creative direction.

## Process

### Step 1: Ask for Selection

After presenting all treatments in Phase 3:

> Which treatment feels most like *your* brand? You can:
> 1. Pick one outright
> 2. Pick one as the base and pull in elements from another
> 3. Tell me what's missing and I'll revise

At most 1 question. Present as a choice among the treatment names.

### Step 2: Handle Hybrid Requests

If the user wants to combine treatments (common and fine):

1. Identify the **base treatment** (the dominant voice)
2. Identify the **borrowed elements** (specific sections from other treatments)
3. Check for coherence — some combinations clash:

| Combination | Works? | Why |
|-------------|--------|-----|
| Treatment A's tone + Treatment B's visual mood | Usually yes | Voice and visuals can flex independently |
| Treatment A's tone + Treatment B's taglines | Risky | Taglines ARE the voice — mixing voices creates dissonance |
| Treatment A's messaging + Treatment B's visual mood | Usually yes | Strategy and aesthetics can pair differently |
| Treatment A's tone + Treatment B's tone | No | Pick one voice. You can't be folksy AND clinical. |

If a combination doesn't work, say so directly:

> Those two tones would fight each other. I'd keep Treatment A's voice and pull in Treatment B's visual direction instead — that gives you [desired quality] without muddying the voice.

### Step 3: Refine the Selected Treatment

Take the selected (or hybrid) treatment and sharpen:

1. **Elevate taglines** — take the best candidate and produce 2 more variations at that quality level
2. **Add context-specific headlines** — write headlines for each primary channel identified in the GTM
3. **Tighten do/don't** — add 1-2 more examples based on the specific product's features
4. **Specify visual direction** — push from mood to more specific direction (e.g., from "warm tones" to "warm clay tones — think terracotta, sandstone, dried grass")

### Step 4: Present Refined Treatment

Show the refined treatment as the final creative direction card with all fields at `[+]` or `[✓]`:

```
## Selected Treatment: [Name] [✓]

[Full treatment card with refinements applied]

### Channel-Specific Applications

#### [Channel 1]: [channel name]
- **Format:** [what content looks like here]
- **Voice adjustment:** [any channel-specific tone shift]
- **Example post/headline:** "[example]"

#### [Channel 2]: [channel name]
- **Format:** [what content looks like here]
- **Voice adjustment:** [any channel-specific tone shift]
- **Example post/headline:** "[example]"
```

### Step 5: Gate Check

**Proceed to Phase 5 when:**
- User has confirmed a treatment (or hybrid)
- Refined treatment is presented

Transition:

> Creative direction confirmed. Let me compile the **full creative brief** — everything in one document you can hand to a designer, copywriter, or use as your own reference.

Then read `workflows/05-summary.md`.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Don't push back on the user's choice.** You recommended; they chose. Respect it even if they didn't pick your recommendation.
- **Don't over-refine.** The direction should be tight enough to execute from, not so prescriptive it kills creative freedom.
- **Don't create a Frankenstein hybrid.** If the user wants elements from 3+ treatments, help them see which 2 are actually compatible.
- **Don't add new sections.** Refinement sharpens what exists — it doesn't bolt on new strategic elements.
